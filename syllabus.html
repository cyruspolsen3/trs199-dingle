Course Description and Purpose

Experience the Dingle Peninsula as both physical landscape and sacred text through an intensive study of Irish pilgrimage traditions, contemporary poetry, and place-based theology. This experiential course combines scholarly analysis with embodied learning as students walk ancient pilgrim routes, engage with local communities, and create original reflective works. Students examine how landscape shapes spiritual practice and literary expression while developing skills in cultural interpretation, comparative religious studies, and creative synthesis.

The course draws on Irish, Celtic, and North Atlantic literary and spiritual traditions — including the desert monastic heritage that shaped the Dingle Peninsula's own díseart communities, the archipelagic imagination of writers from Orkney and Kerry alike, and the Ignatian principle of contemplation in action. The course is rooted in sustained attentiveness to local ecology and the lived traditions of the Dingle Peninsula — the land itself serving as monastery, poem, and pilgrimage.

Handwritten, physically collected June 3, with six dated entries integrating site observation, passage citation, and personal reflection. Sketches, pressed materials, GPS notes, and weather observations are encouraged. Handwriting is not an arbitrary constraint: it slows the mind, roots attention in the body, and produces an irreplaceable record of your particular encounter with the Peninsula.

A printable field journal template (PDF) covering all six required entries, with AI audit trail forms and submission checklist, is available on the course resources page.

Policies & Expectations
AI Audit Trail Protocol

The AI Audit Trail is not a restriction on AI use. It is a practice of authorial accountability and a form of reflective writing in its own right — entirely consistent with this course's commitments to contemplation, discernment, and honest encounter with place.

  • Field Journal, Site/Poem Analysis, Creative Portfolio note: Append or annotate 2–3 specific instances where AI was consulted — what you asked, what it produced, what you changed, and why. Where AI was not used, a single declaration suffices.
  • Poetry and creative writing: If an AI-generated line or image was retained, name it and offer a brief artistic justification. This reflection is poetic and critical work, not a penalty.
  • Lightning Talk (oral): One sentence of verbal acknowledgment at the opening. Five seconds.
  • Post-Course Reflection: One paragraph on AI use across the whole course — what you gained, what you resisted, and what that resistance taught you about the limits of AI and the irreplaceability of embodied presence.

The Audit Trail does not penalize AI use. It penalizes concealment. A student who acknowledges using AI extensively but demonstrates genuine authorial judgment is practicing exactly the kind of reflective discernment this course cultivates.

Community Engagement & Cultural Protocols

Practice cultural humility and seek consent before recording any interactions. Learn basic Irish phrases and proper place-name pronunciations. Support local businesses and adhere to Leave No Trace principles. Sacred spaces require particular reverence; follow all guidance provided by site managers. Academic integrity remains paramount — accurate citation of poetry and primary sources is required in all written work.

Accessibility & Support

Alternative routes are available for all hiking activities, with van support on extended walking days. Students requiring modified assignments due to mobility limitations will receive appropriate alternatives without penalty, including adapted performance formats for the Creative Portfolio. Contact Dr. Olsen before June 3 to discuss any accessibility needs. Daily wellness check-ins with faculty help ensure all participants remain healthy and supported.

Safety & Risk Management

Weather-based go/no-go criteria determine whether outdoor activities proceed as planned. A buddy system and 12:1 student-faculty ratio are maintained at all times. Faculty carry emergency contacts, first aid supplies, GPS devices, and incident logs. EU emergency contacts and travel insurance verification are in place for all participants.

Late Work & Grade Appeals

All written and creative work must be submitted by stated deadlines to receive full credit. Late work may be penalized unless prior arrangements are made for legitimate reasons. No extra credit is offered; one revision of the Site / Poem Analysis written component is permitted if discussed with Dr. Olsen before June 4. Grade appeals follow university policy and must be initiated through the appropriate departmental process.
